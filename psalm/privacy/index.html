Psalm Privacy Policy

Psalm was built to know as little about you as possible. This policy explains what the app does, and mostly what it doesn't do, with your information.

Effective 16 October 2026. Last updated 17 October 2026.

The short version

  • No ads, no advertising trackers, no profiling. Nothing about you is ever sold or shared for marketing.
  • You can use everything in Psalm without an account. From version 1.1 you can choose to sign in with Apple; what that stores is described below.
  • The Bible and every day's readings are inside the app. Psalm does not fetch Scripture from the internet.
  • Once a day, Psalm sends one anonymous request so we can count how many people use it. It is described in full below.
  • One third party helps us count subscriptions and see which parts of the app help. It is named below, with exactly what it receives, and it never learns your name or email address.
  • We never use anything you do in Psalm to train any AI model.

What stays on your device

What you read, the verses you save, your streak, your colours and your notification times are kept on your iPhone and are never sent to us. The few facts about how the app is set up that are counted, such as your church, are listed below, each with where it goes.

The daily count

Once a day, the app or one of its widgets sends a single request to our server. It says:

  • which church you picked in Psalm (Catholic, Orthodox New Calendar, Orthodox Old Calendar, other), or that you haven't picked one,
  • which Psalm widgets are on your Home Screen and Lock Screen, and
  • the app's version and your iOS version.

Like any internet request, it arrives with your IP address, from which our hosting provider (Supabase) notes the country. The request carries no name, no account and no identifier for you or your device, and we store none of it ourselves: it exists only in our hosting provider's standard request logs, which are deleted after a short time. We use it only to count how many people use Psalm each day, and which churches and widgets they use. It is never linked to you and never used for advertising or tracking.

If you sign in

Signing in is optional and adds nothing you need for daily use — it exists so future features can keep your saved verses across devices. If you choose Sign in with Apple (from version 1.1), we store:

  • an opaque user identifier issued by Apple, and
  • the email address associated with it, which may be Apple's private relay address if you chose to hide yours.

That is the whole list. It is held by our authentication provider, Supabase, in an account system used only by Psalm, and used only to operate your account. You can delete your account inside the app — open Settings and scroll to the bottom for Delete Account — and deletion removes it from our systems.

Subscriptions

Psalm Premium is billed by Apple through your Apple Account. We never see your payment details, and whether you have Premium is decided on your device from Apple's own record of what you bought, not by any server of ours.

To know how many people subscribe, we report completed purchases to RevenueCat, a subscription-analytics provider. What RevenueCat receives:

  • the purchase record Apple issued — which plan, when, whether it is still active, and the country of your Apple Account;
  • a random identifier for your installation of the app;
  • if you are signed in, the same account identifier we use in our own database, so that a subscription can be matched to an account when you write to us for help; and
  • from version 1.1, a few facts about how the app is set up, held against that random identifier — or against your account identifier if you are signed in: how many Psalm widgets are on your Home Screen and Lock Screen, whether notifications are allowed, which typeface is chosen, the answers given on the welcome screens (including your church) and how far through them you went, and how often the Premium screen was opened, from where and for roughly how long, and which of its two designs you were shown. They tell us which parts of the app help and which get in the way.

RevenueCat is never given your name, your email address or your payment details. If you sign out or delete your account, your installation goes back to reporting under a random identifier only.

None of this decides what you can read. Every word of Scripture in Psalm is free, and always will be.

Advertising for Psalm

Psalm shows no ads. We do advertise Psalm itself on the App Store, and an ad is only worth paying for if we can tell whether it brought anyone. So from version 1.1, when the app is first opened it asks Apple whether the install followed a tap on one of our App Store ads. Apple's answer names the campaign and the search term, never the person, and it is kept by RevenueCat against the random identifier described under Subscriptions.

Psalm does not ask for permission to track you, does not use your device's advertising identifier, and includes no advertising company's software. None of this is used to show you ads inside Psalm, because there are none.

Notifications

Verse notifications are scheduled on your device and do not pass through our servers.

Children

Psalm does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included.
